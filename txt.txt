# THE LAST WAY OUT — Gameplay, Animation, Environment and Rendering Refinement

You are working on an existing Three.js browser-based horror game. Your task is to inspect the current implementation, identify the actual causes of the issues below, and implement robust corrections.

Do not blindly rewrite working systems. First inspect the project structure, current controls, vehicle implementation, character rigs, camera system, interaction manager, animation system, and rendering pipeline. Preserve existing gameplay and story logic unless a change is explicitly required below.

Do not merely add visual effects to hide incorrect behaviour. Fix the underlying implementation.

## 1. Scooter / Motorcycle Acceleration and Riding Physics

The vehicle currently accelerates unrealistically, reaching approximately 0–100 speed in one second. Correct this.

Implement gradual, believable acceleration:

- Starting the engine must not immediately move the vehicle.
- The vehicle should accelerate progressively from rest.
- Use a realistic acceleration curve rather than instantly assigning a high velocity.
- Engine response should depend on throttle input.
- Include a gradual increase in engine RPM and engine sound.
- Include a sensible maximum speed.
- Braking should reduce speed progressively.
- Releasing the throttle should allow the vehicle to decelerate naturally.
- Turning should feel appropriate to the current speed.
- Avoid instantaneous changes in velocity or rotation.
- Add appropriate engine vibration and vehicle movement without excessive camera shake.

Use delta time and a consistent physics update. Avoid frame-rate-dependent movement.

If a physically accurate simulation is unnecessary, use a tuned arcade-physics model with believable acceleration, braking, steering, and inertia.

The vehicle should feel like a real scooter or motorcycle, not a character moving on top of a sliding object.

## 2. Wind, Speed Effects and Riding Visibility

As the vehicle accelerates, environmental wind should become progressively stronger.

Implement speed-dependent effects:

- Low speed: almost no wind distortion.
- Moderate speed: subtle wind noise and mild environmental movement.
- High speed: stronger wind audio and restrained peripheral blur.
- Very high speed: slightly reduced peripheral clarity, but the central viewing area must remain readable.

Do not blur the entire screen aggressively. The player must still be able to see the road and obstacles.

Wind audio should scale with vehicle speed, not play at a constant volume.

The effects must respond smoothly when accelerating and decelerating. Avoid sudden changes.

## 3. Helmet and Visor — Realistic Geometry and Visibility

The current helmet implementation needs a complete inspection and correction.

Study the real geometry and coverage of a full-face motorcycle helmet before modifying the model.

The helmet should have:

- A rounded outer shell.
- A correctly proportioned chin bar.
- Side coverage around the cheeks.
- Rear coverage around the back of the head.
- A realistic neck opening.
- A properly positioned visor.
- A visible visor hinge or pivot region.
- Appropriate thickness around the shell.

The visor must cover the intended eye opening. Do not make the entire helmet transparent or leave an unrealistic large opening.

The player's visibility should be calculated from the actual helmet geometry and visor state.

### Visor behaviour

Implement:

- Visor open.
- Visor closed.
- Smooth opening and closing animation.
- Correct visor position and rotation.
- Appropriate transparency and reflections.
- A subtle tint, if appropriate.
- Slightly reduced peripheral visibility when the visor is closed.
- Clear central vision through the visor.

The visor must not make the entire world excessively dark or blurry.

When the player wears the helmet, the camera should behave as though the player is looking through the actual eye opening and visor.

Do not simulate helmet coverage by simply applying a large black overlay.

If the player removes the helmet, the restricted helmet-view effect must disappear.

### Helmet pickup and placement bug

Currently, after picking up a helmet, it sometimes resets to its original position or becomes invisible.

Fix the underlying object-state and transform-management issue.

Requirements:

- A helmet must have one authoritative state.
- Its world transform must be preserved when picked up or dropped.
- Its parent transform must be handled correctly.
- Pickup must not create duplicate helmet instances.
- Dropping must restore the correct world position and rotation.
- The helmet must remain visible after interactions.
- The helmet must not reset unless the game explicitly restores a saved state.
- Its collision and interaction state must match its visible state.

Inspect local/world transform conversion, parent-child relationships, object pooling, and interaction cleanup.

## 4. Road Reconstruction

The current road needs improvement.

Create a believable four-lane road section that gradually transitions into a narrower two-lane road near the forest.

The road should not suddenly change width.

Requirements:

- Four lanes in the initial developed-road section.
- Clearly defined lane markings.
- Believable lane widths.
- Road shoulders.
- Appropriate roadside barriers or drainage where relevant.
- Roadside vegetation.
- Natural road curvature.
- Subtle surface imperfections.
- Correct road-to-ground alignment.
- Smooth transition into a two-lane forest road.

The road should have two nearby forested sides as the player approaches the forest.

The forest should feel like a continuous landscape, not a flat green wall beside the road.

Include varying tree heights, trunk thicknesses, shrubs, ground cover, and vegetation density.

## 5. Traffic and Broken Vehicles

The road currently feels empty.

Add a restrained, believable amount of traffic and roadside activity.

Possible vehicles:

- Scooters.

## 6. Input Mapping and Interaction Conflicts

This must be fixed systematically.

Do not solve it by adding another hard-coded exception.

Create a central input and interaction priority system.

Requirements:

- A clear distinction between vehicle controls and world interactions.
- No simultaneous activation of conflicting actions.
- The highest-priority valid interaction should be selected.
- Interaction prompts must reflect the action that will actually occur.
- Input handling must account for the current player state.

| Player state | E key action |

| Standing near petrol pump | Interact with petrol pump |
| Near an interactable door | Knock or open |
| Near a helmet | Pick up helmet |
| Near an inactive vehicle | Start engine, if appropriate |

Do not allow an interaction to execute merely because the player is inside a trigger radius. Require appropriate targeting or context.

Also inspect `Escape` key conflicts throughout the game.

`Escape` must not simultaneously:

- Close a menu.
- Confirm a decision.
- Cancel an interaction.
- Trigger a game action.
- Close a dialogue.
- Pause and unpause the game.

Create a consistent input priority hierarchy.

Suggested priority:

1. Active modal dialog or decision.
2. Active inventory or phone interface.
3. Pause menu.

5. Browser-level behaviour, where applicable.

The Escape key should normally close the highest-priority open interface or pause the game. It must not accidentally confirm decisions.

Use explicit input ownership and prevent event propagation where necessary.

### Materials and textures

- Reuse materials.
- Use texture atlases where appropriate.

- Near: detailed model.
